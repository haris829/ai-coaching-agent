# UC-04 — Assumptions register

Everything UC-04 invented because the company has not specified it. One row per assumption, each
with the risk if it turns out to be wrong and where it lives in code.

Check the rows marked **Verify before integrating** against the real system *before* writing an
adapter. They change what an adapter has to do.

| ID | Area | Assumption | Why | Risk if wrong | Where in code |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A-01 | Non-repetition | A new explanation counts as a repeat of an earlier one when Jaccard similarity of their content-token sets reaches **0.65**. | Fitted to measured data on this runtime: the six real framings peak at 0.512 similarity to each other, a substantive reword measures 0.733. 0.65 sits between, with 0.138 headroom below and 0.083 above. | Too low: legitimate framings are rejected as repeats and the learner hits exhaustion early. Too high: reworded repeats pass as new. Neither is unsafe; both degrade quality. | `core/thresholds.py`, `core/fingerprint.py`; pinned in `tests/test_thresholds.py` |
| A-02 | Non-repetition | Lexical similarity cannot catch a heavy reword that swaps in fresh vocabulary, and we do not pretend it can. The **structural** guarantee is the framing registry handing out each strategy once. | A token-overlap measure has no access to meaning. Claiming otherwise would misrepresent the control. | A generator that both ignores its framing instruction *and* rewords heavily could repeat itself in substance. Mitigated by rejecting any result whose `framing_used` differs from the one requested. | `core/fingerprint.py`, `core/framing.py`; boundary demonstrated in `tests/test_explain_differently.py::test_the_paraphrase_detector_has_a_stated_lexical_limit` |
| A-03 | Framing set | The six strategies are `first_principles`, `analogy`, `worked_example`, `contrast_near_miss`, `procedural_walkthrough`, `misconception_correction`, tried in that order. | The brief gave these as examples; nothing specifies the real set or an order. First principles first because it is the most generally useful opening. | A pedagogically wrong order gives a worse first answer. The set being wrong means "explain differently" cycles through approaches the company would not have chosen. | `domain/enums.py` (`FramingStrategy`, `FRAMING_ORDER`) |
| A-04 | Framing exhaustion | After all six are spent for a concept in a session, UC-04 says so and offers to go deeper or move on. It **never** reuses a strategy. | The brief requires honest exhaustion and forbids silent cycling. Reuse would also make "explain differently" an unbounded enumeration over lesson material. | If the company wants unlimited re-explanation, this reads as a refusal. That trade was made deliberately: reuse reopens the content-extraction path (A-10). | `core/framing.py`, `core/coaching_service.py::_exhausted_response` |
| A-05 | Retrieval | Section identification uses IDF-weighted lexical overlap over the lesson's own sections, with a match threshold of **0.35**, plus two guards: a query term must hit a title/concept name/keyword (not body prose alone), and either two terms must match or the question must name a concept outright. | No vector database is permitted and none is needed to pick a section from one lesson. Deterministic and reproducible. | Under-matching sends in-lesson questions to the general-knowledge path (visible, honest, but unhelpful). Over-matching attributes an answer to a section that does not cover it. | `core/section_matcher.py`, `core/thresholds.py` |
| A-06 | Vocabularies | The concept vocabulary is 14 legal-education concepts across 5 topics, authored here. | Tags must come from a closed list or later aggregation is meaningless. The company has not supplied its taxonomy. | Almost certainly wrong in detail. Every real question will tag `unclassified` until the real taxonomy replaces this file. The unclassified rate is logged so the mismatch is visible immediately. | `domain/vocabularies.py` |
| A-07 | Vocabularies | Tagging matches the longest in-vocabulary surface form present in the question, and `unclassified` when nothing matches. | Deterministic and inspectable. A tagger that guesses defeats the point of a closed vocabulary. | A question naming two concepts is tagged with the more specific one, which may not be the one the learner meant. | `adapters/mock/concept_tagger.py` |
| A-08 | Quiz protection — **Verify before integrating** | The Courses Agent **exposes lesson quiz items** (stem, options, key). Known-item matching depends entirely on this. | Signal 1 of the two required signals is deterministic matching against real items. Without items there is nothing to match. | **If quiz items are not exposed, UC-04 drops from two independent signals to one.** Detection then rests on intent classification alone, `quiz_detection_confirmed` is always `null`, and the deterministic half of the control is simply absent. This is the single most consequential unknown in this component. | `domain/models.py::QuizItem`, `core/quiz_protection.py`; the no-items path is tested in `tests/test_quiz_protection.py::test_known_item_matching_is_unavailable_when_the_lesson_exposes_no_items` |
| A-09 | Quiz protection | The known-item match threshold is **0.85** Jaccard over content tokens. | High on purpose. At 0.85 the item's own stem matches and a same-topic question does not; at 0.30 an ordinary hearsay question matches the hearsay quiz item, which would make every topical question a quiz. | Too low: ordinary questions are treated as quiz items, and `quiz_detection_confirmed` becomes meaningless. Too high: reworded quiz questions escape signal 1 (signal 2 still applies). | `config.py` (`QUIZ_MATCH_THRESHOLD`), `core/quiz_protection.py`; both edges pinned in `tests/test_thresholds.py` |
| A-10 | Content extraction | Verbatim lesson material leaves only through a budget: at most **3 distinct spans per concept per session**, **2 per response**, **25 words per span**, drawn only from concept definitions and curated key points. Section body prose is **never** quotable. | An explanation is a transformation of source material, not a projection of it. Without a budget, "explain differently" enumerates the lesson. | Too tight: explanations lose grounding. **Residual, stated plainly:** a concept's own definition is largely unavoidable when explaining that concept, so a learner who asks about N concepts can recover up to N definitions. Measured on the fixture: 0/11 body sentences, 6/15 key points, 4/4 definitions of the concepts actually asked about, 33% of all source units. | `core/extraction.py`, `core/thresholds.py`; measured in `tests/test_extraction.py` |
| A-11 | Explanation depth | `LEVEL_3`/`LEVEL_4` → basic, `LEVEL_5`/`LEVEL_6` → intermediate, `LEVEL_7`/`LEVEL_7_PLUS` → advanced. **Levels 4 and 6 are the assumed halves of these pairs.** | The platform contract fixes the three profiles; grouping 4 with 3 and 6 with 5 is our reading. Level 6 is an undergraduate law degree, not Masters level, so it is deliberately not advanced. | A Level 4 learner gets a basic explanation they may find patronising, or a Level 6 learner gets an intermediate one when the company intends advanced. Low blast radius, easy to change in one dict. | `domain/enums.py::NARIC_LEVEL_PROFILE`; asserted per level in `tests/test_calibration.py` |
| A-12 | Quiz protection | The intent classifier bands are: **≥ 0.55** answer-seeking, **≥ 0.30** ambiguous, below that concept-learning. The ambiguous boundary moved from the reference implementation's 0.28. | At 0.28 on this runtime, "Which one should I pick?" fell into plain concept-learning. It is genuinely ambiguous and belongs in the middle band, where the answer is still given but the turn is flagged. | Too low: ordinary questions are flagged ambiguous and logged as tuning candidates, adding noise. Too high: genuinely ambiguous questions pass unflagged. Neither withholds help — both bands still explain the concept. | `core/thresholds.py`, `adapters/mock/quiz_intent.py`; pinned in `tests/test_thresholds.py` |
| A-13 | Interaction record | `quiz_detection_confirmed` means: `true` a known lesson quiz item matched; `false` intent fired, the lesson does carry items, and none matched (a suspected false positive); `null` no detection, or the lesson exposes no items so confirmation was impossible. | The field name is specified; its semantics are not. Tri-state lets a consumer tell "refuted" from "could not be checked", which a boolean cannot. | A consumer reading `false` as "definitely not a quiz question" would be over-reading it. Documented in SHARED_CONTRACT.md for exactly that reason. | `core/quiz_protection.py::assess` |
| A-14 | Interaction record | `question_class` is one of `concept_explanation`, `quiz_answer_seeking`, `out_of_lesson`, `ambiguous`. | The field is specified; its vocabulary is not. These four are what UC-04 can actually distinguish. | A consumer expecting different values gets unusable data. Cheap to remap. | `domain/enums.py::QuestionClass` |
| A-15 | Privacy | `question_text` on the interaction record is written as `[redacted:not_persisted]`, never the learner's words. | The field is in the contract shape, but the record is queried by concept and lesson. Storing the question adds re-identification risk without analytical value. | If the company needs question text for tuning, this must change — and it becomes a data-protection decision, not an implementation one. | `core/privacy.py`; asserted in `tests/test_logging.py` |
| A-16 | Lesson content shape — **Verify before integrating** | A lesson has: sections with an id, title, prose body, curated key points and concept tags; concepts with a tag, name, one-sentence definition, owning section and keywords; optional quiz items. | Nothing specifies the shape. This is the minimum UC-04 needs to identify a section, name a concept, and explain within budget. | **The key points and the one-sentence definitions are load-bearing.** They are the only quotable material. A content source without them produces "the lesson does not set this out in enough depth" for every question — correct behaviour, useless product. | `domain/models.py::LessonContent`; the sparse case is `tests/test_grounding.py::test_a_sparse_section_does_not_produce_a_lesson_grounded_claim` |
| A-17 | Cross-lesson references | `get_course_structure` returns **every** lesson in the course. It is the whitelist references are verified against. | Verification needs a complete list of what exists. | An incomplete list silently strips real references (safe, invisible). A list containing ids that do not exist would let unresolvable references through (not safe). This asymmetry is why the conformance kit checks the structure contains the lesson being taught. | `core/cross_lesson.py`, `conformance/courses.py` |
| A-18 | Session identity | UC-04 receives `session_id` and never creates one. A blank one is refused rather than minted. | The platform owns session lifecycle. `ALLOW_DEV_SESSION_IDS` exists for standalone runs and defaults off. | If some caller expects UC-04 to open a session, that caller breaks — loudly, with `session_required`. Preferable to silently orphaning interaction records under an invented id. | `core/coaching_service.py::_require_session`; `tests/test_resilience.py` |
| A-19 | Enrolment | If the enrolment service is **unavailable**, UC-04 does not load lesson content but still answers from general knowledge with an explicit notice. Only a definite "not enrolled" is refused outright. | Unverifiable is not the same as unauthorised. General knowledge is not the company's IP, so withholding it protects nothing. | If the company requires a hard refusal whenever enrolment cannot be verified, this is one branch to change. | `core/coaching_service.py::_load`; `tests/test_enrolment.py` |
| A-20 | Follow-up actions | `go_deeper` re-explains the same concept one profile step further (basic → intermediate → advanced), does **not** spend a framing strategy, and is not a difficulty signal. | The action is named in the API but not defined. Depth and approach are different axes, and depth is capped by the ladder so it cannot enumerate content. | If `go_deeper` is meant to mean something else (a longer answer, a new sub-topic), this is one method to change. | `core/coaching_service.py::go_deeper` |
| A-21 | API validation | Unknown request fields are **rejected** with 422 and named in `rejected_fields`. | §8 requires unknown fields to produce a visible validation error rather than a silent ignore; a review of the reference implementation separately required that a caller can see a field had no effect. Rejecting-and-naming satisfies both: nothing a client sends can vanish quietly. | A caller sending a harmless extra field gets a 422 rather than having it ignored. Deliberate: for a component where a caller might try `disable_quiz_protection`, loud is better than lenient. | `api/schemas.py`, `api/app.py`; `tests/test_api.py` |
| A-22 | Identity | The principal is read from the `x-user-id` header. | A placeholder for whatever the platform gateway injects. No production authentication is in scope. | None while it stays a placeholder. It must be replaced before any real deployment; it is a registry entry like any other adapter. | `adapters/mock/current_user.py`, registry key `CURRENT_USER_PROVIDER` |
| A-23 | Casing | Values the platform contract names literally keep its form: NARIC levels stay `LEVEL_3`…`LEVEL_7_PLUS`, while `grounding`, `naric_level_source`, `rating_state` and the source-status vocabulary are lowercase. Everything UC-04 invented is lowercase. | The contract writes them that way. Applying a blanket lowercase rule would have produced `level_3`, which the contract does not use. | If the platform actually wants lowercase NARIC values, this is one enum. | `domain/enums.py`; asserted in `tests/test_api.py::test_every_emitted_enum_value_is_lowercase_except_the_naric_level` |
| A-24 | Source status | Status is reported **per dependency** (`enrolment`, `lesson`, `course_structure`, `learner_context`, `concept_tagger`), not once for the whole turn. | A turn can have a healthy lesson and a dead context service; one status cannot express that. | A consumer expecting a single scalar has to pick a key or reduce the map. | `domain/models.py::CoachingResponse.source_status` |
| A-25 | Generator | `ConfiguredAnswerGenerator` is wired but has no transport. It raises `ProviderUnavailable` until an integrator fills it in. | Writing the call would mean inventing an external API, which the brief forbids. | None while `ANSWER_GENERATOR=fake`. Setting it to `configured` without implementing the call fails loudly, which is the intended behaviour. | `adapters/generators/configured.py` |
| A-27 | Course/lesson identity — **Verify before integrating** | `course_id` and `lesson_id` arrive in the **request body**, not resolved from `session_id`. | The specified port set has no way to resolve a session to its linked course and lesson: `LearnerContextProvider.get_context(session_id, user_id)` returns NARIC level and practice area only. Something has to supply the linkage, and the caller is the only source available. | **Enrolment is the control, and it holds**: the learner must be enrolled on whatever course they name, verified server-side on every request. The residual is narrower — a learner could address a *different lesson within a course they are already enrolled on*. That is not privilege escalation, but it is not the "session arrives linked to a lesson" model of §4.1 either. **If the platform wants the linkage server-side, it needs a port that resolves `session_id -> (course_id, lesson_id)`.** That is one new port and one call in `_load`; nothing else changes. | `api/schemas.py::AskQuestionRequest`, `core/coaching_service.py::_load` |
| A-26 | Tokenisation | Hyphens are split, so "out-of-court" and "out of court" are the same phrase. | Treating them as different tokens made retrieval and paraphrase detection disagree with themselves depending on the author's punctuation. It moved the measured reword similarity from 0.533 to 0.733. | Hyphenated terms of art that should stay atomic are split. None in the current vocabulary are affected. | `core/text.py`; `tests/test_regex_verification.py` |
